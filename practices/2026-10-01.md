## Session log
- **Warmup:** 200m in 3:33.
- **4×100 scull/free:** 1:40, 1:42, 1:43, ~1:56, about 27s rest between. The 4th time is unreliable: the Watch recorded overlapping segments around a pause there.
- **20×50 @ 1:00:** start-to-start held at 56–65s throughout, so the send-off worked as intended (about 15–22s rest per rep).
  - Reps 1–7: 38.8–43.5s, averaging ~41.9s. Rep 8's time is unreliable for the same Watch reason as the 4th 100.
  - Reps 9–16: 41.5–46.9s, averaging ~44.1s. This was the slowest and most uneven block.
  - Reps 17–20: 40.4–41.0s, averaging ~40.6s. The fastest and most consistent block, swum after a ~3min break.
  - Breaks between blocks: ~2:45 and ~3:00.
- **Cooldown:** 200m recorded as 2×100 with about a 1min stop between (1:59, 1:46), then the 2×25.
- **What it means:** every rep was faster than CSS pace. CSS (1.04 m/s) works out to ~48s per 50m, and the reps ran ~40–47s. With ~15–20s rest, that makes this a hard, near-threshold set, not the easy-plus-true-max mix of a Speed Bridge day. That's fine as a one-off, but it's the kind of effort the polarized framework keeps rare.

## Notes
- **Not a Speed Bridge session:** there were no all-out reps with long rest, so this week's Wednesday sprint slot effectively didn't happen. The Day B card's z1 + z4–z5 shape wasn't followed. Per the polarized-vs-threshold RCT in [research_findings.md](../research/research_findings.md) (finding #1), polarized training beat a threshold-heavy split while also causing less fatigue. If more sessions like this one start to pile up, that's the trade-off to watch.
- **The 20×50 looks like USRPT, but the rest was different:** the USRPT study in [research_findings.md](../research/research_findings.md) also used 20×50, but with 1:1 work:rest at race pace. Today's work:rest was about 2.3:1 (~42s work, ~18s rest), so its lower-lactate finding doesn't carry over directly. The claim that USRPT protects technique was refuted, so it shouldn't be read into this set either.
- **Sculling:** this was the second session in a row with sculling, after the 4×50 taste in the [09-28](2026-09-28.md) warmup. No confirmed research ranks sculling (or any specific drill) for race-pace transfer, so it counts as feel work, not something the evidence weighs in on.
