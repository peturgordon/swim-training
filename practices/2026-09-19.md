## Notes
- **Glide trial:** floated after Monday's (09-14) aerobic set ran z3/z4 instead of the prescribed z1/z2, with technique hard to hold while keeping HR down. More glide can lower metabolic cost at a given pace (higher distance-per-stroke, lower stroke rate) — but `research/research_findings.md` also documents that elite swimmers *maintain* (don't increase) intracycle velocity variation as effort rises, and that too much dead time between strokes is itself a marker of *reduced* propulsive continuity — i.e., coasting, not efficiency. This is a real experiment with a real failure mode, not a confirmed fix.
- **How to read the result:** falling stroke count at the same pace/split as 09-12 means the glide is helping; a falling count *with* a slower split means it's just costing speed, not improving efficiency. Pace is being tracked this week for exactly that reason — stroke count alone can't distinguish the two outcomes.
- Continuing this week's high-elbow catch (fingertip-drag) cue from Monday and Wednesday at moderate pace in the Technique block, per the framework's Day C card.
- No fins — as on [2026-09-05](2026-09-05.md) and [2026-09-12](2026-09-12.md), fins measurably shorten/flatten the kick and raise energy cost per meter, cutting against a session testing unaided technique/efficiency under fatigue.
- Pull buoy on 2 of the 4 aerobic-base reps only, not on the tracked 3×200 — full-stroke kinematics are what's being evaluated (and what the glide trial is actually about) in the tracked set.
- Pre-pool prehab (side planks + dynamic hip/ankle mobility, ~5min) per the framework's Day C card — dose isn't validated for this population, treat as prudent general conditioning, not a proven protocol.
- The bpm bands above come from [`research/heart_rate_zones.md`](../research/heart_rate_zones.md) — an age-formula estimate (Tanaka 208−0.7×age, ~178bpm max), not a personally measured zone.
