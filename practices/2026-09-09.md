# 2026-09-09 — Speed Bridge (Sprint)

**60min / 1600m**

## Warmup — 300m
- 200m easy free, relaxed
- 4×25 build (easy → moderate), 15s rest

## Technique — 400m
*(fins, one cue only — continuing this week's high-elbow catch cue from Monday)*
- 4×50 catch-up drill, fins, 15s rest
- 4×50 high-elbow catch (fingertip-drag), fins, 15s rest

## Bridge — 300m
- 6×50 building 25 easy → 25 near-max over the last 15–25m, holding the cue as pace rises, 30–40s rest

## Max effort — 400m
- 8×50: first 25m truly all-out, ease off the remaining 25m back to the wall, 75s rest
- Quality over count — stop the set early (even at 5–6 reps) the moment form visibly breaks down, rather than grinding out all 8

## Cooldown — 200m
- Easy free/back, relaxed

**Total: 1600m**

## Metrics to capture
- RPE (1–10) or a 10s pulse count immediately after the max-effort reps.

## Notes
- Distance is deliberately capped well under Monday's 2300m — a rest-heavy sprint day trades volume for full recovery per rep, not the same meters compressed into shorter rests.
- 75s rest on the max-effort reps, not 10–30s — per [Training Framework](../guides/training-framework.html#framework)'s own audit of the Garpar log, short rest on "genuinely maximal" reps pushes them toward lactate-tolerance conditioning instead of protected speed work; matches [`research/research_findings.md`](../research/research_findings.md)'s USRPT finding that shorter reps at full recovery produce significantly lower blood lactate than longer reps at the same pace and work:rest ratio.
- Fins stay confined to the Technique block, not the max-effort reps — the equipment research found fins measurably change kick mechanics (reduced amplitude, altered coordination, higher energy cost per meter), so heavy fin use teaches a different kick, not a faster version of the same one.
- Pre-pool prehab (banded external rotations + push-up plus, ~5min) is recommended per Training Framework's Day B card — dose isn't validated for this population, treat as prudent general conditioning, not a proven protocol.
- Polarized shape overall (easy + true max effort, one progression block in between), not a sustained moderate-hard middle zone — the research's RCT found a polarized split beat a threshold-heavy one for sprint-event improvement, with less fatigue.
