# Poolside Tools — Metrics Capture & Budget Timing

A product-research pass, run 2026-09-06, resolving two open questions from [`BACKLOG.md`](../BACKLOG.md): what to actually use to capture metrics while training, and what to use for the occasional timed test (the CSS 400m+200m time trial that [Training Framework](../guides/training-framework.html#tracking) recommends every 6–8 weeks).

This is product/price research, not the scientific-claim verification `research/research_findings.md` runs (no adversarial voting) — but the same standard applies: nothing below is stated as fact without a source, and prices/specs are cross-checked across at least two independent listings where feasible. Prices are what was found on 2026-09-06 and will drift; treat them as a ballpark, not a quote.

**Revised 2026-09-06** after Petur flagged that most of the first pass's picks (AquaNotes, SportCount) aren't confirmed available in Iceland or the EU, that the stopwatch doesn't need to be a specialized finger-worn device — a plain stopwatch is fine — and asked specifically for DIY alternatives to AquaNotes. This revision adds an availability check per product, a DIY subsection under Part 1, and replaces Part 2's recommendation with a plain stopwatch actually sold in Iceland. Nothing below is deleted outright — where the original pick turned out to be a poor fit, that's noted in place rather than silently dropped, so the reasoning stays visible.

## Part 1 — Poolside metrics-capture methods

The problem: [Training Framework's tracking section](../guides/training-framework.html#tracking) recommends capturing stroke count, CSS, heart rate, RPE, and SWOLF, but pen and paper doesn't survive pool water, and exact numbers (stroke count per length, splits) aren't reliably reconstructed from memory after a hard set.

### Which metrics need instant-of-capture vs. which survive memory — *reasoned, not sourced*
This split isn't a researched claim, just what follows from what each metric actually is:
- **Needs capture the instant it happens:** stroke count per length, exact split times. These are precise numbers that decay from memory within minutes, especially under fatigue.
- **Reconstructable right after the session:** overall RPE, how a set felt, roughly where heart rate was. These are impressions, not numbers — memory holds up fine for "that felt like an 8" logged 20 minutes later.

### Waterproof slates and wetnotes — *cross-checked, real product, dive-market not swim-market, Iceland availability unconfirmed*
Waterproof notepads exist and are genuinely submersible: [AquaNotes](https://www.myaquanotes.com/aquanotes-waterproof-notepad) is a suction-cup-mounted waterproof notepad with a water-resistant cedar pencil, and the broader dive-slate/wetnotes category ([Paragon Dive Store](https://www.paragondivestore.com/collections/slates-wetnotes), [SCUBAPRO WetNotes](https://scubapro.johnsonoutdoors.com/us/shop/accessories/wetnotes-underwater-notebook)) covers the same need for scuba divers. These are built for a diver treading water or a coach at the wall, not a swimmer mid-stroke — writing still requires stopping and using both hands, so it fits the CSS-test/benchmark-set scenario (write the split down at the wall between reps) better than continuous mid-set logging.

**Iceland/EU availability — checked, unresolved:** AquaNotes' own [shipping policy](https://www.myaquanotes.com/shipping-returns) says it ships to "over 50 different countries" with subsidized international postage, but doesn't name which ones, free shipping is US-only, and import duties are the buyer's responsibility; no EU marketplace listing (Amazon.de/.co.uk/.se) was found. Whether Iceland is actually reachable isn't resolved by searching — it would take checking AquaNotes' own shipping calculator or emailing them directly. Given that uncertainty, the DIY options below are the more reliable route for Petur specifically.

### DIY alternatives to AquaNotes — *mixed confidence, see per-method notes*
Four DIY approaches turned up, at different confidence levels:

- **Permanent marker on skin — *cross-checked, real documented practice, but for a different purpose.*** Writing on the arm with a Sharpie/permanent marker (explicitly *not* pen, which washes off) is a well-documented practice among youth and masters swimmers — [NewSwimmer.com](https://www.newswimmer.com/swim-meets/marking-your-swimmers-arm/), [SCR Sharks](https://scrsharks.swimtopia.com/writing-events-on-your-swimmers-arm), [Centennial Swim Team](https://centennial.swimtopia.com/guide-to-arm-marking). The documented use is marking event/heat/lane before a race, not logging live numbers mid-set — but the underlying fact (permanent marker holds up on wet skin through a swim session, ordinary pen doesn't) transfers directly to writing a stroke count or split on your own arm between reps. No grease pencil use on skin was found in any source.
- **DIY dive slate (scrap plastic + pencil on a cord) — *well documented, easy to build.*** Cutting a small slate from scrap PVC or acrylic (fence offcut, shower-surround PVC, Azek/Versatex composite trim), sanding the surface for tooth, drilling a hole, and tying on a golf-style pencil or mechanical pencil with surgical tubing or a bungee cord is a repeatedly-documented DIY build in the diving community: [Instructables](https://www.instructables.com/DIY-Dive-Slate/), [Make:](https://makezine.com/article/maker-news/diy-dive-slate/), [ScubaBoard forum](https://scubaboard.com/community/threads/diy-slates.362700/), [scuba-diving-smiles.com](https://www.scuba-diving-smiles.com/dive-slate.html). Materials cost is trivial and everything is available from an Icelandic hardware store — this is the closest DIY equivalent to AquaNotes' actual function (a wall-side wipeable slate), just homemade.
- **Grease pencil on a wipeable surface (kickboard, deck tile, laminated card) — *plausible, not directly documented for swim coaching.*** Grease pencils/china markers are confirmed to write on wet, glossy, non-porous surfaces (tile, glass, plastic, vinyl) and wipe off with a damp cloth ([Wikipedia: grease pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grease_pencil)), and a vinyl document-protector product page explicitly recommends grease pencil or a water-based felt pen for notes on wipe-clean vinyl covers ([DraftingSteals](https://www.draftingsteals.com/catalog-portfolios--expanding-wallets--mailing-materials-for-drawings-clear-vinyl-covers-for-drawing.html)). No source was found of a swim coach or swimmer actually doing this on a kickboard or deck tile specifically — the documented poolside-coaching practice instead is a dry-erase clipboard/board ([USMS forum thread](https://community.usms.org/swimming/f/general/3477/writing-workouts-on-dry-erase-board-or-something-else)), which is the same idea (wipeable surface, marker-family pen) in a slightly different, already-commercial form.
- **Rite in the Rain waterproof paper — *real product, reachable from Europe, not stocked in Iceland directly.*** Rite in the Rain paper is chemically treated so an ordinary pencil writes on it even wet, per [Rite in the Rain's own FAQ](https://www.riteintherain.com/faqs) — a genuinely different mechanism from a grease pencil. The manufacturer ships only within North America directly, but lists European resellers: [Any Weather Paper](https://www.anyweatherpaper.co.uk/) (UK), [europeanpaper.com](https://www.europeanpaper.com/rite-in-the-rain.html), and [military.eu](https://military.eu/p/rite-in-the-rain/rite-in-the-rain-waterproof-copier-paper-a4-200-pcs-148697). None of these confirm Iceland specifically, but ordering from a UK/EU reseller is a shorter shipping hop than the US-based manufacturer.
- **Plain pencil + laminate or page protector — *not documented, inference only.*** No source describes ordinary graphite pencil on laminated paper or a plastic sheet protector as a poolside note-taking method. It sits between two documented-but-different facts (RITR's treated paper takes a normal pencil; grease pencil, not graphite, is what's documented on vinyl/laminate) without being either — plausible-sounding, not confirmed, and not recommended above the other options here.

### Swim-tracking smartwatches — *cross-checked, discontinued by Garmin but still sold in Iceland, still not a budget option*
Garmin's swim-specific watches (Swim 2, and swim-capable Forerunner models) automatically capture distance, pace, stroke type, stroke count, and SWOLF per length without any manual input — the watch detects lap turns itself, and Garmin Connect (the companion app/cloud sync) is optional, not a subscription requirement. **Correction to the first pass:** Garmin officially retired the Swim 2 around May 2025 — it's no longer sold on garmin.com ([Garmin Rumors](https://garminrumors.com/garmin-swim-2-officially-retired/)) — so the "upgrade path" framing below is now about remaining stock, not a current Garmin product. It's still stocked in Iceland by Garmin's official local retailer, Garminbúðin ([Swim 2 White](https://garminbudin.is/vefverslun/swim-2-swim2-white/), [Swim 2 Black](https://garminbudin.is/vefverslun/swim-2-swim2-black/)), and ELKO also lists Garmin watches generally ([ELKO](https://elko.is/voruflokkar/garmin-ur-266)) — so unlike AquaNotes or SportCount, this one's actually directly buyable in Iceland, price not re-checked at Garminbúðin specifically (US price from the first pass, $220–270, may not hold).

### Smart goggles — *cross-checked, real product, higher price than the watch, has an EU storefront*
[FORM Smart Swim 2](https://www.triathlete.com/gear/swim/triathlete-tests-the-new-form-smart-swim-2-heads-up-display-goggles/) project a heads-up display inside the goggle lens showing real-time pace, splits, stroke rate, stroke count, distance per stroke, and heart rate (with a compatible strap) while swimming — the only option here that surfaces numbers *during* the set rather than after it. Priced at $250 for the standard model, with a cheaper LT variant at $149 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2164698/form-smart-swim-2-lt/), [Triathlete review](https://www.triathlete.com/gear/swim/form-smart-swim-2-lt-goggles-review)) — those are US prices from the first pass. FORM does run a dedicated EU storefront ([eu.formswim.com](https://eu.formswim.com/products/smart-swim-2-goggles)) with free shipping to Germany, Spain, France, Italy, and the Netherlands specifically named; Iceland isn't named but the site says to check shipping per-country at checkout, so it's plausibly reachable, just not confirmed. Still more expensive than the watch and a bigger commitment than what Petur asked for here — noted for completeness, not recommended.

### Voice memo + waterproof phone pouch — *plausible, not verified against pool-specific use*
A phone in a submersible pouch (e.g. IPX8-rated pouches from [Hiearcool](https://www.amazon.com/Hiearcool-Waterproof-Phone-Pouch-Underwater-2/dp/B079HV3TC9) or similar, reviewed by [Paddling Magazine](https://paddlingmag.com/gear/accessories/bags-boxes-cases-packs/waterproof-phone-pouches/) and [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/cell-phones/best-waterproof-phone-cases-and-pouches-a1202666146/)) left at the wall could take a voice memo right after a set. This is a workable idea, not something the search verified anyone actually doing for swim training specifically — the pouches themselves are real and cheap (commonly $10–20), but "leave it at the wall, talk into it wet-handed between reps" is inference, not a confirmed practice.

### Training partner or coach noting — *not researched, self-evident*
Someone poolside not swimming that rep can write down what a slate or memory can't capture in real time. No product research needed; the limiting factor is whether one is available, not whether the method works.

## Part 2 — Budget waterproof stopwatches/watches for occasional lap timing

The problem: not a daily-wear tracker or a subscription ecosystem — something inexpensive, genuinely waterproof, usable to time the CSS test and similar benchmark swims every few weeks. **Revised 2026-09-06:** Petur said this doesn't need to be a specialized finger-worn device — a plain stopwatch is fine — and the original top pick (SportCount) turned out to have unconfirmed Iceland shipping. The order below now leads with what's actually buyable in Iceland today.

### TYR Z-200 (Aquasport, Iceland) — *cross-checked, buyable directly in Iceland today, new top pick*
An ordinary coach-style stopwatch, not worn while swimming — hang it on the lanyard, start it at the wall, catch the split when you touch — sold directly by [Aquasport](https://aquasport.is/vefverslun/sundlaugavorur/timaklukkur-sundlaugar/skeidklukka-z200-stop-watch/), an Icelandic swim-equipment specialist retailer (confirmed via their own product catalog: swimwear, goggles, paddles, kickboards, lane lines, starting blocks — a dedicated aquatics shop, not a general sporting-goods site). 8,590 ISK. Described on the product page as "vatns- og höggþolin smíði" (water- and shock-resistant construction — no numeric ATM/meter rating given), with a 60 dual-split automatic memory, a built-in stroke-frequency counter, and configurable entry/exit timing — explicitly marketed for swim training and racing ("tilvalið fyrir bæði þjálfun og keppni"). This is exactly "a regular stopwatch," purpose-sold for pool use, with no import shipping, customs, or currency conversion involved.

### SportCount LapCounter & Timer — *cross-checked, real product, Iceland shipping unconfirmed*
A finger-worn, one-button device built specifically for lap swimmers: press it once per length/lap while actually swimming, no need to stop. Waterproof to 50m, $39.95–44.95 direct from the manufacturer depending on model (details unchanged from the first pass — see Sources). **Availability correction:** SportCount Europe, the authorized EU dealer, ships to the UK and "European countries" via free Royal Mail Airmail, but [their own site](https://sportcount-europe.com/about/) doesn't name Iceland specifically, and a post-Brexit import-fee caveat applies to non-UK European orders — so whether it reaches Iceland cleanly isn't resolved by searching alone. Since Petur doesn't need the finger-worn form factor anyway, this is now a secondary option rather than the lead pick.

### Timex Ironman Classic 30 — *unchanged from first pass, Iceland/EU availability not re-checked*
A conventional digital sport watch, water resistant to 100m, with manual stopwatch/lap/split functions via 5 buttons — no automatic lap detection, a button press at each wall. ~$45–50 in the US ([Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/TIMEX-Men-s-IRONMAN-Classic-30-Black-Gray-38mm-Sport-Watch-Resin-Strap/55532011), [Timex](https://timex.com/products/ironman-classic-30-full-size-resin-strap-watch-t5h591)). Timex is a global brand likely sold through Icelandic watch/electronics retailers, but this pass didn't verify a specific Icelandic listing — treat as an unconfirmed fallback, not a checked recommendation.

### Decathlon Fastime/W500M — *cross-checked, real product, no direct Iceland storefront*
Decathlon sells plain waterproof digital stopwatches (5 ATM, interval/lap mode) under its Fastime/W500M line ([Decathlon UK](https://www.decathlon.co.uk/accessories/stopwatches), [W500M listing](https://www.decathlon.com.gr/en/p/328138-183336-second-life-w500m-running-stopwatch-blue.html)) — the same "plain stopwatch" category Petur asked about. Decathlon has no Iceland storefront or direct shipping there; reaching Iceland would mean a package forwarder ([Ubuy Iceland](https://www.ubuy.is/en/brand/decathlon), [ColisExpat](https://www.colisexpat.com/en/delivery-shipping/iceland/decathlon/)), which adds cost and time the Aquasport option doesn't need.

### Ultrak 495 — *cross-checked, same product category as the Z-200, no Iceland availability confirmed*
A coaching stopwatch (100 dual-split memory, large 3-row display, 5-year battery), water-*resistant* rather than built for submersion. $65–69 ([yourswimlog.com](https://www.yourswimlog.com/best-stopwatches-swim-coaches/), [Dick Pond Athletics](https://www.dickpondathletics.com/ultrak-495-stopwatch/)) — pricier than the Z-200 for the same kind of device (a wall-side stopwatch, not worn while swimming), with no Iceland source found. Superseded by the Aquasport find below, not a distinct category as the first pass framed it.

### Recommendation
For "a regular stopwatch to time laps once in a while," the **TYR Z-200 from Aquasport** (8,590 ISK) is the clear pick: it's the only option here confirmed buyable in Iceland with no import step, it's exactly the plain wall-side stopwatch Petur asked for (not a specialized finger-worn device), and it's purpose-marketed for swim training with a stroke-frequency counter as a bonus. The SportCount, Decathlon, and Timex options are kept above as fallbacks in case the Z-200 is ever out of stock or Petur wants to compare, but none of them currently beat "sold directly in Iceland today" on this specific ask. Garmin Swim 2 remains noted in Part 1 as the automation upgrade path if the budget constraint is ever lifted — and, per the correction above, it's also directly available in Iceland via Garminbúðin, even though Garmin itself has discontinued the model globally.
