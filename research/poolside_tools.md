# Poolside Tools — Metrics Capture & Budget Timing

A product-research pass, run 2026-09-06, resolving two open questions from [`BACKLOG.md`](../BACKLOG.md): what to actually use to capture metrics while training, and what to use for the occasional timed test (the CSS 400m+200m time trial that [Training Framework](../guides/training-framework.html#tracking) recommends every 6–8 weeks).

This is product/price research, not the scientific-claim verification `research/research_findings.md` runs (no adversarial voting) — but the same standard applies: nothing below is stated as fact without a source, and prices/specs are cross-checked across at least two independent listings where feasible. Prices are what was found on 2026-09-06 and will drift; treat them as a ballpark, not a quote.

---

## Part 1 — Poolside metrics-capture methods

The problem: [Training Framework's tracking section](../guides/training-framework.html#tracking) recommends capturing stroke count, CSS, heart rate, RPE, and SWOLF, but pen and paper doesn't survive pool water, and exact numbers (stroke count per length, splits) aren't reliably reconstructed from memory after a hard set.

### Which metrics need instant-of-capture vs. which survive memory — *reasoned, not sourced*
This split isn't a researched claim, just what follows from what each metric actually is:
- **Needs capture the instant it happens:** stroke count per length, exact split times. These are precise numbers that decay from memory within minutes, especially under fatigue.
- **Reconstructable right after the session:** overall RPE, how a set felt, roughly where heart rate was. These are impressions, not numbers — memory holds up fine for "that felt like an 8" logged 20 minutes later.

### Waterproof slates and wetnotes — *cross-checked, real product, dive-market not swim-market*
Waterproof notepads exist and are genuinely submersible: [AquaNotes](https://www.myaquanotes.com/aquanotes-waterproof-notepad) is a suction-cup-mounted waterproof notepad with a water-resistant cedar pencil, and the broader dive-slate/wetnotes category ([Paragon Dive Store](https://www.paragondivestore.com/collections/slates-wetnotes), [SCUBAPRO WetNotes](https://scubapro.johnsonoutdoors.com/us/shop/accessories/wetnotes-underwater-notebook)) covers the same need for scuba divers. These are built for a diver treading water or a coach at the wall, not a swimmer mid-stroke — writing still requires stopping and using both hands, so it fits the CSS-test/benchmark-set scenario (write the split down at the wall between reps) better than continuous mid-set logging.

### Swim-tracking smartwatches — *cross-checked, but not a budget option*
Garmin's swim-specific watches (Swim 2, and swim-capable Forerunner models) automatically capture distance, pace, stroke type, stroke count, and SWOLF per length without any manual input — the watch detects lap turns itself. [Garmin Swim 2](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/665374/) is waterproof to 5 ATM, and Garmin Connect (the companion app/cloud sync) is optional for basic on-watch functionality — no subscription required to use the core swim-tracking features. Current price found at $220–270 across retailers (Amazon low end ~$220–234, Walmart ~$219–231, list $269.99) — [Garmin official product page](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/665374/), [price tracking via camelcamelcamel](https://camelcamelcamel.com/product/B07XHLF7TV). This automates exactly the "instant capture" metrics (stroke count, splits) that are otherwise hard to log — but it's a standing investment and a step up from "occasional," not the budget stopwatch Part 2 covers.

### Smart goggles — *cross-checked, real product, higher price than the watch*
[FORM Smart Swim 2](https://www.triathlete.com/gear/swim/triathlete-tests-the-new-form-smart-swim-2-heads-up-display-goggles/) project a heads-up display inside the goggle lens showing real-time pace, splits, stroke rate, stroke count, distance per stroke, and heart rate (with a compatible strap) while swimming — the only option here that surfaces numbers *during* the set rather than after it. Priced at $250 for the standard model, with a cheaper LT variant at $149 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2164698/form-smart-swim-2-lt/), [Triathlete review](https://www.triathlete.com/gear/swim/form-smart-swim-2-lt-goggles-review)). More expensive than the watch and a bigger commitment than what Petur asked for here — noted for completeness, not recommended.

### Voice memo + waterproof phone pouch — *plausible, not verified against pool-specific use*
A phone in a submersible pouch (e.g. IPX8-rated pouches from [Hiearcool](https://www.amazon.com/Hiearcool-Waterproof-Phone-Pouch-Underwater-2/dp/B079HV3TC9) or similar, reviewed by [Paddling Magazine](https://paddlingmag.com/gear/accessories/bags-boxes-cases-packs/waterproof-phone-pouches/) and [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/cell-phones/best-waterproof-phone-cases-and-pouches-a1202666146/)) left at the wall could take a voice memo right after a set. This is a workable idea, not something the search verified anyone actually doing for swim training specifically — the pouches themselves are real and cheap (commonly $10–20), but "leave it at the wall, talk into it wet-handed between reps" is inference, not a confirmed practice.

### Training partner or coach noting — *not researched, self-evident*
Someone poolside not swimming that rep can write down what a slate or memory can't capture in real time. No product research needed; the limiting factor is whether one is available, not whether the method works.

## Part 2 — Budget waterproof stopwatches/watches for occasional lap timing

The problem: not a daily-wear tracker or a subscription ecosystem — something inexpensive, genuinely waterproof, usable to time the CSS test and similar benchmark swims every few weeks.

### SportCount LapCounter & Timer — *cross-checked across two independent sources, best fit*
A finger-worn (adjustable strap, not a wrist watch), one-button device built specifically for lap swimmers: press it once per length/lap while actually swimming, no need to stop or use both hands. The plain LapCounter tracks lap count only; the LapCounter & Timer and LapCounter & Timer 200 add split/lap timing (up to 200 stored splits on the "200" model), reporting total time, fastest/slowest split, and average pace. Waterproof to 50m depth — confirmed independently via [a lap-counter buying guide](https://www.yourswimlog.com/best-swim-lap-counters/) and product listings ([SwimOutlet](https://www.swimoutlet.com/products/sportcount-combo-lap-counter-and-timer-4027), [tallycounterstore](https://tallycounterstore.com/products/sportcount-lapcounter-timer-200-water-proof)). Prices direct from [the manufacturer's product list](https://www.sportcount.com/sc-products/): LapCounter $39.95, LapCounter & Timer $42.95, LapCounter & Timer 200 $44.95 — all under $50, no subscription, purpose-built for exactly this use case (occasional benchmark-swim timing while actually swimming, not poolside coaching).

### Timex Ironman Classic 30 — *cross-checked, a normal watch worn while swimming*
A conventional digital sport watch, water resistant to 100m (fine for swimming, not diving), with manual stopwatch/lap/split functions via 5 buttons — no automatic lap detection, you press a button yourself at each wall. Priced around $45–50 at Walmart ([Walmart listing](https://www.walmart.com/ip/TIMEX-Men-s-IRONMAN-Classic-30-Black-Gray-38mm-Sport-Watch-Resin-Strap/55532011), [Timex official product page](https://timex.com/products/ironman-classic-30-full-size-resin-strap-watch-t5h591)) across several case-size variants at the same price point. A reasonable alternative to the SportCount if Petur would rather it look and function like an ordinary watch outside the pool too, at the cost of needing a free hand at the wall to press it — same friction the SportCount is specifically designed to avoid.

### Ultrak 495 — *cross-checked, but the wrong form factor for this use case*
A coaching stopwatch (100 dual-split memory, large 3-row display, 5-year battery), water-*resistant* rather than built for submersion — reviews describe it surviving an accidental drop into a pool, not being worn while swimming. Priced $65–69 ([Amazon-sourced roundup](https://www.yourswimlog.com/best-stopwatches-swim-coaches/), [Dick Pond Athletics](https://www.dickpondathletics.com/ultrak-495-stopwatch/)). This is a poolside-coach tool (someone standing at the wall timing a swimmer) — the wrong shape for Petur timing himself solo, and pricier than the two options above for a worse fit.

### Recommendation
For "time laps once in a while to track progress" without a subscription or a daily-wear commitment, the **SportCount LapCounter & Timer** (~$43, or the 200-split version at ~$45) is the best match: cheapest of the genuinely-submersible options, built exactly for a swimmer timing their own reps mid-swim (one-handed press, no stopping), and directly fits the CSS 400m+200m time-trial format the framework already calls for. The Timex Ironman is a solid fallback if Petur wants something that reads as a normal watch the rest of the day. Garmin Swim 2 remains the upgrade path if the budget constraint is ever lifted — it's the only option here that also automates the Part 1 metrics (stroke count, SWOLF) rather than just timing.
